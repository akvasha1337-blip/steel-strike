Steel Strike wiring guide

Six wireless steel targets: ESP32-C3 nodes with piezo sensors and LEDs, an ESP32 hub at the shooting line, and the Steel Strike web app. Print this page or keep it open on a phone while you build.

How it fits together

Each steel plate has a piezo disc that feels the impact. A small ESP32-C3 in a box behind the plate reads it, flashes the plate’s LEDs instantly, and radios the hit to the hub at the shooting line using ESP-NOW (no router needed). The hub passes hits to the laptop over USB, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, and the web app runs the game.

Tools

Soldering iron and solder, multimeter, wire strippers, heat-shrink tubing and a lighter or heat gun, small drill for cable glands, side cutters, a rubber mallet for testing.

Sensor circuit (one per plate)

A piezo disc hit by a bullet impact can produce tens of volts. These three parts make that safe for the ESP32 and give a clean signal.

Mounting on the plate

  • Clean the bonding spot to bare metal. Glue the piezo brass-side-down with two-part epoxy and let it cure fully. A ring of neoprene around it reduces ringing.
  • If plates take heavy hits, bond the piezo to the hanger bracket or a small steel tab bolted to the stand instead of the plate itself. The stand carries the vibration well and the sensor lives longer.
  • Keep the piezo lead short (under 50 cm) and use shielded cable, shield to GND at the box end only.
  • Strain-relieve every cable with a gland or zip tie so a hard hit never pulls on solder joints.

Bench test first

Assembly, step by step

Tick steps as you go (ticks are not saved).

  1. 1. Flash the hub: open the hosted app in Chrome, plug the DevKit in by USB, Connect page → Install hub firmware.
  2. 2. Flash each C3 board the same way with Install target firmware. Write a number 1–6 on each board with a marker.
  3. 3. Bench test one C3 on a breadboard (Figure 8). Connect the app over USB, open Setup, tap the steel offcut and watch the meter jump.
  4. 4. Cut the LED strip into 8-LED pieces on the copper pads. Solder three wires (5V, DIN, GND) to the input end — the arrows point away from it.
  5. 5. Solder the piezo circuit (Figure 2) onto a small piece of perfboard: 1 MΩ across the piezo leads, 10 kΩ in series, Zener from signal to GND with its band on the signal side.
  6. 6. Wire the C3 (Figure 3): signal → GPIO0, LED data via 330 Ω → GPIO10, LED 5V and GND to the 5V and GND pins, 1000 µF across the LED supply (stripe = minus).
  7. 7. Optional: 100 k / 100 k divider from 5V to GND with the middle to GPIO1 for supply voltage in the app.
  8. 8. Insulate everything with heat-shrink. Fit the board, perfboard and power bank in the ABS box with cable glands for the piezo and LED leads.
  9. 9. Power on the hub, connect the app, and press Pair new target on the Connect page. Then power on one node box: a new node searches for the hub on its own and appears in the Targets list within a few seconds.
  10. 10. Pair the nodes one at a time in plate order 1–6, so the numbers match your layout. To move a node to another hub, hold its BOOT button for 5 s.
  11. 11. At the range, bond the piezos (Figure 6), place boxes behind cover, run LED leads behind the plates, and position the white backer boards.
  12. 12. Calibrate each plate (below) and run a Free Practice round with a mallet before going hot.

Testing and calibration

  1. Multimeter, power off: no short between 5V and GND (should read more than a few hundred ohms). Piezo leads to GND through 1 MΩ should read close to 1 MΩ.
  2. Multimeter, power on: 4.8–5.2 V between 5V and GND on each node. About 2.5 V on GPIO1 if you fitted the divider.
  3. Live signal: Setup → Sensors. The meter should sit near the bottom when nothing touches the plate and jump high when you strike it.
  4. Calibrate: press Calibrate, keep still for 3 s while it measures noise, then hit the plate. Accept the suggested threshold.
  5. Crosstalk: hit each plate hard and check only that plate registers. If a neighbour also fires, raise its threshold or add rubber between stand and ground.

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
False hits (wind, nearby plates)Raise the threshold; shorten or shield the piezo cable; keep the crosstalk window on (default 5 ms); put rubber under the stand; increase “ignore time after a hit”.
Missed hitsLower the threshold; re-bond the piezo to bare metal with epoxy; move it closer to where bullets land; check the Zener isn’t reversed (band to signal).
Double hits from one shotRaise “ignore time after a hit” to 200–300 ms. Steel rings for a while after a hit.
LEDs flicker or show wrong coloursJoin all grounds; fit the 1000 µF capacitor; keep the data wire short; add the 74AHCT125 level shifter.
Node shows offlinePower bank switched itself off — turn on its low-current mode, or keep keep-alive on in Setup. Check the node is within sight of the hub.
Bluetooth can’t find the hubUse Chrome or Edge (not Safari/Firefox); turn Bluetooth on; on Android allow Location for the browser; disconnect USB/Wi-Fi sessions first.
Wi-Fi app won’t loadType http://, not https://. Try http://192.168.4.1 on the hub’s own network SteelStrike-XXXX. Some phones don’t resolve .local — use the IP shown on the Connect page.
USB port not listedUse a data USB cable (not charge-only). Install the CP210x or CH340 driver on Windows if the board doesn’t show up.
Nodes stop after the hub joins Wi-FiNormal for a few seconds: the hub changed radio channel to match the router and nodes search for it again automatically.

Safety

The range’s rules and the range safety officer come first. This system is a scoring aid. It never controls when the line is hot or cold.

  • Shoot steel only at or beyond the minimum distance in your range rules and the plate maker’s guidance — typically at least 10 m for handgun calibres on AR500, much further for rifles. Only shoot plates rated for the calibre.
  • Plates should be in good condition (no craters or pitting) and tilted so fragments go down into the ground.
  • All electronics, boxes and cables sit behind cover and out of the line of fire. Nothing electronic may be visible from the front of a plate.
  • Never go downrange to adjust a sensor, box or LED while the line is hot. Make changes during a cease-fire only.
  • Everyone near the line wears eye and ear protection, including spectators watching the scoreboard.
